How a plan is made

Forecast

  1. Forecast demand, cautiously

    Sixty weeks of sales feed a model that predicts a likely and a cautious demand for every store and product. Days where a store sold nothing because the shelf was empty are corrected first, so shortages do not shrink next week's forecast.

  2. Choose every shipment at once

    One optimiser weighs freight, lost sales and expiring stock together, across every warehouse, store and truck. It proves the plan is within 1% of the cheapest possible in about six seconds.

  3. Ship the oldest stock first

    A repair pass swaps in the batch that expires soonest wherever shelf life allows, then a checker confirms zero first-expiry-first-out breaks. Warehouse teams get pick lists already in that order.

  4. Rehearse 500 different weeks

    The plan is replayed against 500 simulated weeks of demand, including regional surges. Planners see how a bad week looks and which stores are most likely to run short.

  5. Answer "why not?" with numbers

    Disagree with a decision? Ask for your own shipment. The engine re-plans with it locked in and shows exactly what it costs in freight, expired stock and lost sales elsewhere.
